# Duell — Rules for Play

*(Published by Parker Brothers, 1976, and reissued by Lakeside Industries/Leisure Dynamics, 1976.)*

A head-to-head dice strategy game for 2 players. Tagline: *"A game that uses the ultimate weapon... the mind!"*

Source: transcribed from the original rules sheets (`duall1.webp`, `duall2.webp`, `duall-helper.webp` in this folder).

## Equipment

- An 8x8 checkered board with one square per side embossed as that player's **Key square**.
- 8 dice per side (one full home row), used as the playing pieces. One of them is the **Key die**.
- 6 self-stick foam foot pads (applied to the board's underside, one in each corner — per the original packaging note).

## Setup

Arrange the dice on the two back rows closest to each player, **all with the number 4 facing inward** (toward the opponent), as shown in the starting-position diagram on the box/rules sheet.

One die on the board is each player's **Key piece** — it occupies the embossed Key square (or the square designated as such for that side).

## Determining the First Player

Players determine who starts the match by covering the top number on one of their own dice out in the playing area (hiding their choice from the opponent).

- If the hidden number is **even**, White starts.
- If the hidden number is **odd**, Red starts.

## The Object

Win by either:

1. **Capturing** your opponent's Key piece, or
2. **Landing** your own Key piece on your opponent's Key square.

## Moves

1. A die may be moved the number of spaces indicated by the number showing on its top face at the start of the move.
2. Moves are made by "tumbling" the die over, one space at a time.
3. A move **may change direction once only**, in either direction (forward or backward).
4. Dice may not jump over other dice.
5. Dice may not move diagonally.
6. The **Key piece** may move only **one space per turn**.
7. Once a move is started, it must be completed using the die chosen — you cannot switch to a different die partway through.

## Capturing

An opponent's piece is captured and removed from the game by landing on its space with an **exact count** (i.e., the moving die's number of spaces must land it precisely on the occupied square).

## Playing Hints

1. It's sometimes possible to approach the same square via two different paths.
2. Your choice of route determines what number ends up on the die's "top face" when the move is finished — plan ahead.
3. Because the top number after a move depends on the path taken, you can plan a *subsequent* move by controlling what top number and square your die will end up on. The move diagrams below each show, for a given starting top number, what the resulting top number will be depending on the path (and number of spaces) chosen.

### Reading the move diagrams

The rules sheet includes a set of six diagrams — one for each starting **Top Number** (1 through 6) — showing every square reachable from that die and what the die's new top face will be after moving there. A reference/helper card duplicates these six diagrams with **"SIDE FACING YOU"** labels on all four edges, so the card can be rotated to match your seat at the board regardless of which side you're sitting on. Read each diagram by determining what number is on the side facing you, as marked outside the diagram's border.

These diagrams are preserved as images in this folder:

- `duall2.webp` — Top Numbers 1–6 movement diagrams (original rules sheet)
- `duall-helper.webp` — rotatable reference card with the same six diagrams plus suggested-path arrows

### Machine-parsable move table

[`duell-moves.json`](duell-moves.json) is a complete, structured transcription of all six diagrams, for programmatic use (e.g. a digital implementation). It was generated by simulating standard die-tumbling physics and validating the result against the pip patterns actually printed in `duall2.webp`/`duall-helper.webp` (every reachable square's outcome was checked against the scan, including a global check that all six diagrams agree on a single, consistent die chirality).

Structure: `topNumbers["<1-6>"]` holds the starting die orientation (`top`/`bottom`/`north`/`south`/`east`/`west` face values) and a `squares` array of every square reachable in exactly that many spaces. Each square gives its offset (`dx`/`dy`, in board squares, +dx = east, +dy = south) and one or two `paths` — an ordering of direction+distance segments (e.g. `["N2","E1"]`) together with the `resultTop` value the die shows after completing that exact route. Squares reachable by two different orderings list both, since the route taken determines the resulting top number (see Playing Hints above).

## Winning

The game ends immediately when a player captures the opponent's Key piece, or moves their own Key piece onto the opponent's Key square.
